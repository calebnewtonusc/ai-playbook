# The everyday track

For people who want AI to run real parts of their work and life without writing code. Same rule as the builder track: every level has what the AI does and what you keep doing yourself, and a checkpoint you do without help.

| Level | You can                                        | Read                                                                     | Checkpoint (do it without help)                                                        |
| ----- | ---------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 0     | Think with AI without losing your own thinking | [stay the thinker](../track/00-stay-the-engineer.md)                     | Guess an answer, then ask Claude, and say which was better and why                     |
| 1     | Have a Claude that already knows you           | [level-1-a-claude-that-knows-you.md](level-1-a-claude-that-knows-you.md) | A fresh chat in your Project gets a real task right with no backstory from you         |
| 2     | Hand off a weekly chore                        | [level-2-hand-off-a-chore.md](level-2-hand-off-a-chore.md)               | One chore done by Claude three weeks in a row, with a saved prompt you reuse           |
| 3     | Catch it being wrong                           | [level-3-catch-it.md](level-3-catch-it.md)                               | Find one wrong fact Claude gave you, and say how you caught it                         |
| 4     | Connect your email, calendar and files         | [level-4-connect-your-stuff.md](level-4-connect-your-stuff.md)           | A morning question ("what needs me today?") answered from your real inbox and calendar |
| 5     | Get a real tool built for you                  | [level-5-get-it-built.md](level-5-get-it-built.md)                       | A tool you use every week, built by Claude Code from a spec you wrote                  |

Level 3 is the one people skip. AI sounds equally sure when it's right and when it's wrong, so the skill that matters most is noticing.

## How to know it's working

Once a month, ask yourself:

- What did I stop doing by hand this month?
- Did I catch Claude being wrong at least once?
- Can I still do the important parts myself if Claude were gone tomorrow?
- Do my asks get the right answer on the first try more often than last month?
