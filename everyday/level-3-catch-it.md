# Level 3: catch it being wrong

AI sounds equally confident when it's right and when it's making something up. The people who get the most out of it are the ones who notice.

## Where it goes wrong most

- **Numbers, dates and quotes.** It will produce a plausible one if it doesn't have the real one.
- **Facts about real people and organizations.** Titles, who works where, what someone said.
- **Anything after its training cutoff,** unless it searched the web and showed you where.
- **Summaries of long documents.** It can skip the one line that mattered.

## What you keep doing

- Ask "where did that come from?" on anything you'll repeat to someone else.
- For a fact that matters, open the source yourself. Thirty seconds.
- Tell it the rule up front:

```text
If you don't have a source for a number, date, name or quote, say so and
leave a [CHECK] marker instead of guessing.
```

- When you catch a mistake, add the lesson to your Project's About doc or your saved prompt.

## Make it argue with you

```text
Here's my plan. Give me the three strongest reasons it's wrong, and what
would prove it wrong. Don't be nice about it.
```

## Checkpoint

Find one wrong fact Claude gave you this month. Write down what it said, what was true, and how you caught it.
