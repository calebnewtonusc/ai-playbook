# 0. Start without code

This page is for anyone who wants AI to take real work off their plate and has no interest in learning to code. You'll set up Claude once so it knows who you are, get in the habit of talking to it instead of typing, and hand it your first three jobs.

Everything here works in the Claude app on your phone and computer. No terminal.

## 1. Get the app and the right plan

- [claude.ai](https://claude.ai): sign in, and get **Pro** ($20 a month). Pro gets you Projects, connectors to your email and calendar, and Claude Code later if you want it.
- Install the [desktop app](https://claude.ai/download) on your computer and the Claude app on your phone, signed in to the same account, so a chat you start on one shows up on the other.

If you hit the usage limit often, that's the sign to look at Max. Most people never need it at first.

## 2. Talk, don't type

The fastest way to give AI enough context is to talk for two minutes the way you'd explain something to a smart new assistant. Typing makes people write one short line, and one short line gets a generic answer.

- **In the Claude app**, tap the microphone and talk. It turns what you said into text you can read before sending.
- **Anywhere on a Mac**, turn on dictation (System Settings, Keyboard, Dictation) and press the shortcut it shows to talk into any text box.
- **Want it better than that?** [Wispr Flow](https://wisprflow.ai) is a dictation app a lot of heavy AI users swear by. Optional.

Ramble. Say the backstory, who's involved, what you've tried, what good looks like. Claude sorts it out.

## 3. Make a Project that knows you

A Project is a folder in Claude with standing instructions and files every chat inside it can read. Make one called your name.

Open a new chat in it and say:

```text
Interview me so you can write an "About me" doc for this Project. Ask one
question at a time: what I do, my businesses and roles, the people I work
with most, how I like things written, and what I never want you to do.
When you have enough, write the doc and I'll paste it into the Project.
```

Answer out loud. When it writes the doc, read every line. Fix anything wrong, then paste it into the Project's instructions or add it as a file. Every chat in that Project now starts knowing you.

Update it whenever something changes. A new venture, a new person, a correction. Two minutes now saves you re-explaining yourself in every chat for a year.

## 4. Connect your stuff

In Claude's settings, under **Connectors**, you can connect Gmail, Google Calendar and Google Drive (and more). Once they're on, you can ask things like:

- "What's on my calendar this week, and what do I need to prep for?"
- "Find the email from the venue about the date and summarize what they need from me."
- "Pull the notes doc from last month's meeting and list what we promised."

Start with questions that only read. Let it draft emails, but you read and send them yourself. Nothing goes out under your name that you didn't read.

## 5. Your first three jobs

Pick three things you do every week that are annoying and low risk if they go wrong. Then:

```text
Here's everything I do in a normal week: [talk through it]. Which three could
you take off my plate, ranked by hours saved and how bad it would be if you
made a mistake? For each one, tell me exactly what I'd say to you every time.
```

Do the first one this week. Then the next.

## 6. When you want something built

At some point you'll want a real tool: a contact list that reminds you who to call, a page for an event, an agent that finds grants. That's [Claude Code](https://claude.com/claude-code), which lives on your computer and builds things for you.

You'll hear the word **GitHub**. It's like Google Drive for projects: it keeps a copy of everything Claude builds, with every version saved, so nothing is ever lost. You never have to learn it. Claude Code handles it. You just say "save this to GitHub."

When you're ready, go to [guide step 1](01-tools.md).

## The habit that keeps you sharp

Before you send a big ask, spend 30 seconds guessing what a good answer looks like. When Claude answers, compare. That one habit is the difference between AI making you smarter and AI making you lazy. The rest of it is in [level 0](../track/00-stay-the-engineer.md).
